Myth vs. Fact

Myth

[Placeholder myth: "Psoriasis is just a skin condition. It's not related to how I'm feeling emotionally."]

Fact

[Placeholder fact: "Psoriasis is a systemic inflammatory condition. Psychological stress directly activates inflammatory pathways that can trigger flares."]

Myth

[Placeholder myth: "Feeling anxious or depressed about my psoriasis is a personal weakness, not a medical issue."]

Fact

[Placeholder fact: "Depression and anxiety are recognised comorbidities of psoriasis. They are medical concerns your care team should know about."]

Myth

[Placeholder myth: "There's nothing I can do about the stress-skin connection — it's just how my body works."]

Fact

[Placeholder fact: "Evidence-based mind-body approaches can reduce stress-related flares and meaningfully improve quality of life with psoriasis."]

Key points

Read the key points Hide the key points
  • [Placeholder: Psoriasis is a systemic inflammatory condition — stress is a recognised biological trigger, not a personal failure.]
  • [Placeholder: The skin and nervous system communicate directly; emotional stress can accelerate inflammation.]
  • [Placeholder: Depression, anxiety, and social withdrawal are common in people with psoriasis, and support is available.]
  • [Placeholder: Sharing the emotional impact of psoriasis with your care team helps them deliver more complete care.]
  • [Placeholder: Stress management skills can reduce flare frequency alongside your medical treatment.]

Bring this to your appointment

  • [Placeholder: Have you noticed a link between periods of stress and your psoriasis flares?]
  • [Placeholder: How has psoriasis affected your mood, relationships, or confidence?]
  • [Placeholder: Are there mind-body supports — such as counselling or stress management programs — you'd like to explore?]
  • [Placeholder: Is there anything about how psoriasis affects you that you've been hesitant to mention to your care team?]
