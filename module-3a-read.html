What is Self-Efficacy?

Believing you can manage eczema is the first step to actually managing it.

Self-efficacy means believing in your ability to carry out the actions needed to manage your condition. Research shows that people with higher self-efficacy take more consistent action, stick to routines longer, and recover from setbacks faster.
Self-efficacy in eczema care isn't about being perfect. It's about trusting that your actions make a difference — even small ones, even imperfect ones, even on hard days.
Self-efficacy is built gradually. Through small successes, through support from your care team, and through learning — over and over — that setbacks are a normal part of the process, not a sign that you've failed.

Tracking Your Symptoms & Triggers

Patterns aren't obvious until you start writing things down.

Symptoms

Rate your itch (0–10) daily. Note affected areas and whether skin looks better, same, or worse.

Foods & Products

Log any new foods or skincare products. Patterns from these often take two to four weeks to emerge.

Stress

Rate your stress daily (0–10). For many people, flares appear one to three days after a high-stress period.

Sleep & Activity

Track sleep hours and physical activity. Both affect the skin through their impact on cortisol and immune function.

💡 App tip: EczemaWise (from the National Eczema Association) lets you track all of this on your phone and shows you trends over time.

Building Small, Consistent Habits

Small consistent habits beat big perfect plans every time.

1
Start with one thing

Choose a single specific habit for one week. Don't try to change everything at once. One habit done consistently is worth more than five habits you give up on by Thursday.

2
Habit stacking

Link your new habit to something you already do reliably. Moisturizing right after brushing your teeth means you never have to remember separately — the existing habit triggers the new one automatically.

3
Build gradually

Once one habit is stable, add the next. This builds routines that survive bad weeks instead of falling apart the moment life gets complicated.

Setting SMART Goals

Goals you'll actually keep — because they're specific enough to follow.

S
Specific

Name exactly what you will do. "Moisturize my arms and legs" beats "take better care of my skin."

M
Measurable

How will you know you did it? "Once daily at bedtime" is measurable. "More regularly" is not.

A
Achievable

Set a goal you can realistically reach this week, not in six months. Small and doable beats ambitious and abandoned.

R
Relevant

Connect it to something that matters to you. Why does this goal improve your life right now?

T
Time-bound

Set a clear start and end point. "For the next two weeks" gives you a natural moment to review and adjust.

Dealing with Setbacks

A flare is not a failure. It's information.

Setbacks are a normal, expected part of managing a chronic condition. They don't mean your routine isn't working — they often mean it's being tested by something new.
When a setback happens, the most useful response is curiosity, not judgement. What was different that week? Was it stress, travel, a new product, less sleep, a change in weather?
Treat setbacks as data, not failure. Each one teaches you something that makes your next plan more accurate and more realistic.
💡 The question after a setback isn't "Why did I fail?" It's "What did I learn — and what will I do differently?"

The Way You Talk to Yourself Matters

Your inner voice shapes how you respond — to flares, to setbacks, and to your care.

"I'll never get this under control."

→ Try instead:

"This is hard, and I'm still learning. My actions are making a difference, even when I can't see it immediately."

"I've ruined my skin again."

→ Try instead:

"This flare will pass. What can I do right now to support my skin?"

"I always forget my routine. I'm hopeless at this."

→ Try instead:

"Building a new habit takes time. I can adjust how I set it up so it's easier to remember."

Thinking it Through

Question 1 of 4
You've been managing your eczema carefully for two weeks, feeling confident and in control. Then a tough week at work causes your routine to fall apart. Your skin flares. You feel defeated and think "I knew I couldn't keep this up." According to what we know about self-efficacy:
Exactly. Self-efficacy isn't the belief that things will always go perfectly — it's the belief that your efforts make a difference even when they don't. Rebuilding after a setback is one of the most powerful ways self-efficacy grows over time.

Thinking it Through

Question 2 of 4
You've been tracking your symptoms for three weeks and notice that your eczema consistently flares two days after stressful workdays. You bring this observation to your next appointment. What's the most useful thing to do with this information?
That's it. Tracking gives you data — and data is most powerful when it's shared with your care team. Knowing that stress is a consistent trigger opens up a conversation about stress management strategies, preventative care, and how to time your treatments around high-stress periods.

Thinking it Through

Question 3 of 4
You want to moisturize twice daily but keep forgetting the evening application. Your dermatologist suggests "habit stacking." Which of the following is the best example of this technique?
Correct. Habit stacking links a new habit to an existing one — so the existing habit becomes the automatic trigger. Brushing your teeth is already reliable; moisturizing right after means you don't need a separate reminder. Over time, it becomes as automatic as the tooth-brushing itself.

Thinking it Through

Question 4 of 4
You want to improve your eczema management and your dermatologist asks about your goal. Which of the following is the best SMART goal?
That's the right idea. A SMART goal is specific about what, when, and for how long. "Every evening after my shower" is specific, measurable, achievable, directly relevant to your skin, and time-bound to two weeks. Goals like "manage better" or "try to moisturize more" give you nothing concrete to follow or measure.

Let's Recap! Key Takeaways

  • › Self-efficacy — believing your actions make a difference — is one of the most powerful tools you have for managing eczema.
  • › Tracking symptoms, stress, foods, and products helps you see patterns that are invisible day to day.
  • › Small consistent habits, built one at a time and stacked onto existing routines, outlast big ambitious plans.
  • › SMART goals are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound — not vague wishes to "do better."
  • › Setbacks are part of the process, not proof of failure. Treat them as information and adjust your plan.
  • › An Eczema Action Plan, written with your doctor, gives you a clear path for daily care, early flare signs, step-up treatment, and when to call.
