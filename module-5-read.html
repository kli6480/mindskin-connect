Flare Prevention 101: Staying Ahead During Stressful Times

Stress is a major eczema trigger. During demanding periods — a new school year, deadlines, personal challenges — your skin may be more vulnerable.

  • The Skin-Mind Connection: stress hormones can weaken your skin barrier and heighten itch perception.
  • Adjust Expectations: perfect skincare routines can be hard when stressed. Focus on consistency with the core elements.
The key to flare prevention is adjusting expectations — “good enough” is better than not at all!

Calming Your System: Mindfulness & Meditation

What is mindfulness? Intentionally paying attention to the present moment — thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations — without judgment. It creates space between a trigger (like itch or a stressful thought) and your reaction, helping you cope.

🎧 Audio-guided relaxation: try short 5-minute breathing exercises, or longer 15–20 minute sessions on body awareness and managing itch. Find a quiet space, listen, and allow yourself to relax and observe. (See your Resource Folder.)

Quick Stress Relievers — Under 5 Minutes

Even short bursts of relaxation can make a difference. Try these anywhere:

Mindful Breathing1–5 min

How: Focus on your breath. Notice the air entering and leaving your body. If your mind wanders, gently bring it back.

When: During a work/study break, waiting in line, before a stressful meeting, or when itch escalates.

Quick Body Scan2–5 min

How: Mentally scan your body from toes to head, noticing any tension without judgment. Consciously try to release it.

When: While commuting (if not driving!), during a lunch break, or before sleep.

Sensory Grounding5-4-3-2-1

How: Name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, and 1 you can taste (or a positive thought).

When: If feeling overwhelmed or anxious, to bring yourself to the present moment.

Deep Relaxation: Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)

What is PMR? A technique where you systematically tense and then relax different muscle groups. It helps you become aware of physical tension and learn to release it — reducing overall stress and easing muscle tension.

  • 1. Find a comfortable position (sitting or lying down).
  • 2. Start with your feet: tense for 5–10 seconds, then release completely for 20–30 seconds, noticing the difference.
  • 3. Move up the body — calves, thighs, abdomen, chest, arms, hands, shoulders, neck, face — tensing and relaxing each group.

Please refer to the linked PMR audio tracks in your Resource Folder.

Calming Your System: CBT

What is cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)? A structured, time-limited psychotherapy that helps identify and modify negative thought patterns and behaviours.

  • Shown to reduce AD symptoms — including itch intensity and sleep problems — and to ease stress, anxiety, and depression associated with eczema.
  • Internet-delivered CBT can lead to significant improvements. Both self-guided and clinician-guided CBT are effective.

Nourishing Your Skin & Mind: The Role of Nutrition

While diet isn't a cure for eczema, good nutrition supports overall health — which can benefit your skin and how you feel!

Anti-inflammatory foods

Prioritize leafy greens, berries, turmeric, and omega-3-rich fish.

Hydration

Water helps maintain skin moisture and flush out toxins.

Gut-skin connection

Probiotics (e.g. yogurt, kimchi) can help support your immune system.

Avoid triggers

Monitor processed foods, dairy, or gluten if sensitivities are suspected.

📝 Tip: Keep a food and flare-up journal to track patterns!

Movement for Well-being: Exercise & Your Eczema

Regular physical activity offers many benefits, even with eczema, if managed carefully.

  • Choose activities wisely: opt for lower-sweat options if sweat is a major trigger (walking, yoga, swimming).
  • Dress for success: wear loose, breathable, moisture-wicking fabrics.
  • Post-exercise care: if sweaty, shower with lukewarm water soon after, pat dry, and moisturize immediately.
  • Listen to your body: if your skin is flaring badly or painful, gentler movement or rest is OK too.

Let's Recap! Key Takeaways

  • ›Proactively manage eczema during stressful times by adapting your routine to “good enough” consistent care.
  • ›Use quick bursts of mindful breathing, body scans, and sensory grounding — plus techniques like PMR or CBT — to calm your system and reduce reactivity to itch.
  • ›Support your skin and overall health with a balanced, anti-inflammatory diet and good hydration.
  • ›Engage in regular, “skin-smart” physical activity to boost mood, reduce stress, and improve well-being.
  • ›Small, consistent wellness habits are powerful tools for living well with eczema.
